The Lifegiver - Salmon

Ecological Significance

Salmon are a keystone species. Their role in ecosystems is crucial in maintaining balance.

  • Salmon carcasses provide nitrogen and phosphurus to surrounding wetlands and forested areas.
    • Trees along salmon-bearing waterways can receive up to 70 percent of their nitrogen from salmon, and the growth rate can be increased three times.
    • Bears will carry the salmon into the forests distributing nutrients further throughout the land.
  • Food source for various species.
    • Salmon can be 33-94% of a bear's annual protein.
    • Birds rely on salmon carcasses for winter survival and migration.
    • They provide nutrients for larger fish and marine mammals.
  • Juveniles balance insect populations
    • About 50% of a juvenile salmonid's diet consists of small invertebrates. This helps keep insect populations balanced.

    • Salmon connect freshwater, ocean, and land ecosystems. When salmon populations decline, it disrupts the natural balance. Many plant and animal species lose important sustenance, while streams, rivers, and forests become less productive and biodiversity declines. Urban development has led to the loss of many salmon-bearing streams in Vancouver, leaving only three wild salmon tributaries remaining today.

Musqueam Cultural Significance

Salmon hold deep cultural significance for the Musqueam people.

  • Salmon are not viewed merely as a resource, but as relatives and teachers. In Musqueam teachings, they are known as life-givers among the animals.
  • The salmon life cycle teaches that life is always moving, evolving, and renewing. Each stage of life has a purpose.
  • Through their journey to the ocean and return home, salmon teach us reciprocity by giving back to the land, waters, and beings.

The land is our oldest Elder. If the land and the inhabitants are lost, the stories and knowledge connected to those places and animals will be, too.

Protect the Salmon

Protecting salmonid species means protecting waterways and the web of connections they support. By protecting salmon, we are also protecting the land and waters that we all depend on to survive. For this reason, we have a collective responsibility to care for our home and the animals.
