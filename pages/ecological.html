Salmon Keystone Species

Salmon are a keystone species. Their role in ecosystems is crucial in maintaining balance.

  • Salmon carcasses provide nitrogen and phosphurus to surrounding wetlands and forested areas.
    • Trees along salmon-bearing waterways receive up to 70 percent of their nitrogen from salmon, which can increase their growth rate three times the average.
    • Bears carry salmon into the forests distributing nutrients further throughout the land.
  • Food source for various species.
    • Salmon can be 33-94% of a bear's annual protein.
    • Birds rely on salmon carcasses for winter survival and migratio
    • They provide nutrients for larger fish and marine mammals.
  • Juveniles balance insect populations
    • About 50% of a juvenile salmonid's diet consists of small invertebrates. This helps keep insect populations balanced.

Reference

Hocking, M. D., & Reimchen, T. E. (2009). The ecology of terrestrial invertebrates on Pacific salmon carcasses. https://morganhocking.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/hocking-et-al-2009.pdf
